Training Note: This is an extract of the key clauses relevant to deposit dispute claims. The full agreement runs to 28 pages. Only clauses directly relevant to the dispute scenarios are reproduced here.
Clause 4 — Payment of Rent Cl. 4
4.1 The Tenant shall pay the Rent of £700.00 per calendar month in advance on the 1st day of each month throughout the tenancy.
Payment shall be made by bank transfer to the account nominated by the Landlord or Agent. No other method of payment shall be accepted without prior written agreement.
Time is of the essence in relation to payment of Rent. Late payment may result in the service of a formal notice and may affect the Tenant's credit record.
Clause 11 — Damage and Liability Cl. 11
11.1 The Tenant shall be responsible for any damage to the Property, its fixtures, fittings, or contents caused by the Tenant, members of the Tenant's household, or their guests, beyond fair wear and tear.
Fair wear and tear means the reasonable deterioration of the Property resulting from normal daily use and the passage of time. It does not include damage caused by negligence, misuse, accident, or failure to report maintenance issues.
Where replacement of a damaged item is required, the Landlord shall apply an age-related deduction to reflect the remaining useful life of the item at the end of the tenancy (the betterment principle). The Tenant shall not be required to fund an improvement beyond like-for-like replacement.
Training Note — Betterment Clause: Clause 11.3 explicitly acknowledges the betterment principle. Where this clause is present and submitted as evidence, the adjudicator will expect the PM to have already applied betterment deductions in their claim. Submitting a full replacement cost without a betterment calculation weakens the case.
Clause 14 — Cleanliness and Condition at Vacation Cl. 14
14.1 The Tenant shall keep the Property, including all fixtures, fittings, appliances, and contents, in a clean and well-maintained condition throughout the tenancy.
At the end of the tenancy the Tenant shall return the Property in the same state of cleanliness as evidenced by the check-in inventory and accompanying photographic schedule.
If the Property is not returned in the required condition, the Landlord or Agent may arrange professional cleaning at the Tenant's cost, deductible from the deposit.
The Tenant shall ensure that all appliances, including the oven, hob, and extractor fan, are cleaned to a professional standard prior to vacation.
Training Note — Clause 14.2: Note that this clause explicitly references the check-in inventory and photographic schedule as the baseline standard. Submitting this clause as evidence is strong — it directly links the cleaning obligation to the baseline documentation. If the check-in inventory has no photographs, the clause itself is weakened.
Clause 16 — Redecoration Cl. 16
16.1 The Tenant shall not carry out any redecoration of the Property without the prior written consent of the Landlord or Agent.
The Tenant shall not be responsible for the cost of redecoration that is attributable to fair wear and tear or the natural expiry of the decoration's useful life. The standard decoration lifecycle is acknowledged to be between 5 and 7 years.
Where redecoration is required as a result of damage beyond fair wear and tear, the Tenant shall be liable for a proportionate share of the redecoration cost calculated by reference to the remaining decoration lifecycle at the end of the tenancy.
Clause 19 — Garden and Exterior Maintenance Cl. 19
19.1 Where the Property includes a garden or exterior space, the Tenant shall maintain such areas in accordance with the Garden Maintenance Schedule attached as Appendix B to this Agreement.
The Tenant shall mow the lawn at least once per fortnight during the growing season (April to October), maintain all hedges and borders, and keep the garden free from weeds and debris throughout the tenancy.
Failure to maintain the garden in accordance with Appendix B may result in the Landlord arranging remediation works at the Tenant's cost, deductible from the deposit.
Training Note — Garden Clause: This is a strong, specific garden clause with an attached maintenance schedule. Compare this to a vague clause that only says "maintain in reasonable condition" — the specificity of Clause 19.2 and the reference to Appendix B is what makes a garden deduction claimable.
