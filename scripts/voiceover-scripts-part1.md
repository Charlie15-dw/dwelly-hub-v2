# Dwelly Property Management Training — Voiceover Scripts (Part 1)

> **Usage:** These scripts are designed to be read aloud while recording screen walkthroughs of the Dwelly portal. Follow the [SCREEN] markers to know what should be visible on screen. Pause at [PAUSE] markers to allow time for clicking and navigation. Highlight [TIP] callouts verbally with extra emphasis.

---

## Script 1: Introduction — Dwelly & Your Portal

**Estimated Duration:** 4–5 minutes

---

[SCREEN: Dwelly logo or branded splash slide]

Welcome to Dwelly. This training will walk you through everything you need to know as a Property Manager on the Dwelly platform. By the end of this session, you will feel confident navigating the portal, managing your daily tasks, and keeping your portfolio running smoothly.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Dwelly Home Screen showing the main navigation]

So what does Property Management actually cover at Dwelly? In short, you are responsible for the day-to-day operational health of your properties. That includes maintenance and repairs, compliance certificates, property inspections, move-out processes, tenant and landlord support, and right to rent checks. It is a broad remit, but the portal is built to guide you through every step.

[PAUSE]

The first thing to understand is that each Property Manager owns a specific portfolio of properties. These are your properties. You are the point of contact, and you are accountable for everything that happens within that portfolio.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Dwelly Home Screen — highlight Properties, Property Management, and My Jobs in the left-hand menu]

Let us look at the Home Screen. There are three areas you will use constantly. First, the Properties tab, which gives you access to every property in the system. Second, the Property Management tab, where all active jobs live. And third, My Jobs, which is your personal task list.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: An example job open on screen]

Now, let us talk about Jobs and Tasks. A job is any piece of work tied to a property. It could be a leak reported by a tenant, a gas safety certificate due for renewal, a scheduled inspection, or a move-out process. Each job follows a structured workflow from start to finish.

[TIP] Think of My Jobs as your daily to-do list. Your goal every single day is to clear it by end of day. If your My Jobs list is empty at five o'clock, you have had a productive day.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: A single job open, showing the full job screen layout]

Let us break down what you see when you open a job. At the top, you have the Flow — that tells you what type of job this is. Next to it is the Job ID, which is the unique reference number. Below that, you will see the Property Address, the Deadline for the task, and the Current Stage the job is at.

Scroll down and you will find the Contacts section, which shows the tenant, landlord, and any providers involved. Then there are Modes, which control how the job progresses. You also have Chat, which is your communication hub. And then the action buttons: Put on Hold, Assign Progress, Change Task Manager, and This Property Tasks.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: A filtered view of Property Management showing different flow types]

Every job belongs to a Flow. A flow is the type of work being done. The flows you will encounter are Compliance, Maintenance, Inspection, Move Out, Support, Right to Rent, and Rent Arrears. Each flow has its own set of stages and rules.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: A job showing stage progression]

Speaking of stages, think of them like a pizza delivery tracker. You place an order — that is stage one. Then the kitchen starts cooking — stage two. Then it is on its way — stage three. And finally it arrives — stage four. Jobs work the same way. Each stage represents a clear step in the process, and the system moves the job forward as actions are completed.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Mode selector on a job — showing Auto, Pause, and Manual]

Finally, let us talk about Modes. Every job runs in one of three modes. Auto means the system handles reminders, chases, and progression automatically. Pause means the job is on hold, waiting for something specific. Manual means you are driving every step yourself.

[TIP] Property Managers who use Auto mode consistently are our top performers. Auto mode does the heavy lifting for you — it sends reminders, chases responses, and keeps things moving. Use it wherever you can.

That is your introduction to the Dwelly portal. Next, we will walk through how to navigate the system in detail.

## Script 2: General Navigation

**Estimated Duration:** 6–8 minutes

---

[SCREEN: Browser with VPN indicator visible, address bar empty]

Before you can access the portal, make sure your VPN is switched on. Without it, you will not be able to reach the system. Once your VPN is connected, open your browser and go to panel.dwelly.team.

[TIP] Bookmark this URL right now. You will be opening it every single day, so save yourself the typing.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Dwelly login page]

Enter your credentials and log in. Depending on your role, you will have a different level of access. There are four levels: Manager, which is the standard Property Manager view; Supervisor, which adds oversight tools; Admin, which includes configuration options; and Superadmin, which has full system access. Most of you will be working at the Manager level.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Dwelly Home Screen — left-hand navigation menu fully visible]

Welcome to the Dwelly Home Screen. On the left-hand side, you will see the main navigation menu. This is your control centre. Everything you need is accessible from here. Let us work through each section.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Global Search bar at the top of the screen]

At the very top, you have the Global Search. This is one of the most powerful tools in the system. You can search for a property by address, a tenant by name, a landlord, or a service provider. It searches across everything.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Search results for a landlord name, then click through to the landlord profile and click the Properties button]

Here is a useful trick. If you search for a landlord and open their profile, you will see a Properties button. Click that, and it shows you their entire portfolio — every property they own that we manage. This is extremely helpful when you need a quick overview of a landlord's holdings.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Properties tab open, showing the status filter or column]

Now let us look at the Properties tab. This lists every property in the system. Each property has a status, and you need to understand what these mean. Prospect means we are in early discussions with the landlord. New means the property has been added but is not yet live. Published means it is listed and available. Let Agreed means a tenant has been secured. Tenanted means the property is occupied and active. Withdrawn means it has been taken off the market. And Offboarding means we are in the process of ending our management of that property.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: A single property open, showing the tab bar across the top: Property, Maintenance, Ledger, Publications, File Manager, Deals, Offers, Notes, Fees]

When you open an individual property, you will see a row of tabs across the top. The Property tab shows the core details — address, landlord, tenant, key dates. The Maintenance tab shows all repair jobs linked to that property. The Ledger tab shows the financial history. Publications shows any listings. File Manager is where documents and certificates are stored. Deals shows the tenancy agreements. Offers shows any offers received. Notes is for internal comments. And Fees shows the fee structure for that property.

[TIP] Get comfortable with the Property Information Screen. You will use it constantly to pull up details, check documents, and review history.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Finance tab in the left-hand menu]

Below Properties in the menu, you will find the Finance tab. This gives you access to financial information, invoices, and payment records. You will use this less frequently than the other tabs, but it is there when you need it.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Property Management tab open, showing the full job list with filters visible]

Next is the Property Management tab. This is where all jobs live — every maintenance request, compliance task, inspection, and move-out across the entire system. You can filter this list by mode, by stage, by flow type, by assigned user, or by agency. Use these filters to drill down to exactly what you need.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: My Jobs tab open, showing a list of tasks sorted by urgency]

And here is My Jobs. This is your personal daily task list. It shows only the jobs assigned to you, sorted by urgency. The most pressing tasks appear at the top. As we said earlier, your goal is to work through this list and clear it by end of day.

[TIP] Warning — once a job is closed, it cannot be reopened. Double-check that everything is complete and documented before you close any job. If you close it prematurely, you will need to create an entirely new job to pick up where you left off.

That covers general navigation. You now know how to log in, search for anything in the system, navigate properties and jobs, and manage your daily task list.

## Script 3: Maintenance — Creating a Repair Request

**Estimated Duration:** 5–6 minutes

---

[SCREEN: My Jobs or Property Management tab showing existing maintenance jobs]

Let us walk through the maintenance process. This is one of the most common workflows you will handle as a Property Manager. We are going to split this into two parts. In this section, we will cover how a maintenance job is created and how to get landlord approval. In the next section, we will cover how to action the repair itself.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Tenant portal view or a newly appeared maintenance job in the queue]

Maintenance jobs can appear in two ways. The first is automatic. When a tenant submits a repair request through the tenant portal, a maintenance job is created in the system automatically and lands in your queue. The second way is manual creation, which you will do when a request comes in by phone, email, or during an inspection.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Property Management tab — click Add Request button]

To create a job manually, go to the Property Management tab and click Add Request. From the dropdown, select Maintenance Request.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: The maintenance request creation form — address field, job name, description, file upload]

You will be prompted to enter the property address. Start typing and select the correct property from the list. Then give the job a clear, descriptive name — for example, "Leaking kitchen tap" or "Broken bathroom extractor fan." In the description field, add as much detail as you have. Include what the issue is, where in the property it is located, and any relevant context. If you have photos or videos from the tenant, upload them using the file attachment option.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Click Create Job — the new job appears on screen]

Once everything is filled in, click Create Job. The system will generate the job with a unique Job ID and place it into the maintenance flow.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: The job open at Step 2, showing the landlord consent stage]

Now here is a critical point. Step two is to obtain landlord consent before taking any action. You must not instruct a contractor or schedule a visit until the landlord has approved the work. This is not optional — it is policy.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Job Chat section open, composing a message to the landlord]

Use the Job Chat to communicate with the landlord. Write a clear message explaining the issue, what the tenant has reported, and ask for their approval to proceed with a repair. Include any photos or details you have. All communication through Job Chat is logged and timestamped, which gives you a full audit trail.

[TIP] Always use Job Chat for landlord communication, never personal email or text. If there is ever a dispute, the chat log is your evidence that you followed the correct process.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: The orange tab visible on the job, clicking it to set Auto mode]

Once the landlord responds with approval, click the orange tab on the job and switch it to Auto mode. This tells the system that consent has been granted, and it can begin automating the next steps — sending provider instructions, reminders, and follow-ups.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: The approval dropdown showing "Approved" option]

In some cases, the landlord may already have standing preferences on file — for example, they may have pre-approved all maintenance under a certain value, or they may have a blanket consent in place. If that is the case, you can select Approved directly from the dropdown without needing to contact them.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: A job where the landlord uses their own provider — showing the "Put on Hold" and contact sharing flow]

There is one more scenario to be aware of. Sometimes a landlord prefers to use their own contractor rather than one of our providers. When that happens, your job is to share the tenant's contact details with the landlord so they can coordinate directly. Then put the job on hold. The landlord's contractor will carry out the work, and once it is done, the TC — the task closer — will close the job on the system.

That completes the first half of the maintenance process. You now know how to create a repair request and secure landlord approval before moving forward.

## Script 4: Maintenance — Actioning the Maintenance Task

**Estimated Duration:** 8–10 minutes

---

[SCREEN: A maintenance job at Step 3 — the scheduling stage]

Welcome back. In the previous section, we created a maintenance job and obtained landlord consent. Now we are going to walk through how to action the repair — from scheduling the visit through to completion.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Job at Step 3 — task description field visible]

Step three is scheduling the visit. Before you instruct a provider, check whether a quotation is needed. If the landlord has requested a quote before committing to the repair, update the task description to include the words "Quote required." This tells the provider that they are attending to assess and quote, not to carry out the work immediately.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Service category and provider selection dropdowns]

Now you need to instruct a provider. Select the appropriate service category — for example, plumbing, electrical, or general maintenance. Then choose a provider from the list. The system will show you available providers for that category.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: The two submission buttons — "Submit and Launch Triggered Flow (Auto)" and "Submit and Keep Paused"]

Once you have selected your provider, you have two options. The first is Submit and Launch Triggered Flow, which puts the job into Auto mode. The system will contact the provider, request available dates, and send reminders automatically. The second is Submit and Keep Paused, which means you will handle the provider communication manually. In almost all cases, use the Auto option.

[TIP] Always prefer Submit and Launch Triggered Flow. Auto mode handles provider chasing for you, saving you significant time across your portfolio.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: A job where the provider has not responded — showing the automated reminder timeline]

What happens if a provider does not respond? The system handles this automatically in Auto mode. It sends three reminders over a five-day period. If the provider still has not responded after five days, the job will flag for your attention. At that point, you can either try the same provider again using the "Resume waiting for visit date" option, or select a different provider entirely.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Job at Step 4 — visit date confirmation stage]

Step four is visit date confirmation. Once the provider responds with an available date, that information needs to be entered into the system. There are three ways this can happen. First, the provider may call in, and the system records the date via a masked call. Second, the provider may reply by email, and the system picks it up. Third, you may need to enter the date manually based on a phone conversation or message.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Confirmation notifications and SMS reminder settings]

Once the visit date is confirmed, the system takes care of the rest. It sends confirmation to the tenant and the provider, and it sends an automatic SMS reminder twenty-four hours before the visit. You do not need to do anything here — just make sure the date is in the system accurately.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Job at Step 5 — Result and invoice stage, showing the outcome options]

Step five is where you record the result and upload the invoice. After the provider has attended, you need to update the job with the outcome. There are three possible results.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: "Job completed" outcome selected, showing the payment options]

The first outcome is that the job was completed successfully. If the repair is done, you need to record who is paying. There are three options: "We pay for landlord," which means the cost comes out of the landlord's account with us; "Landlord pays directly," which means the landlord settles with the provider themselves; or "Tenant pays," which applies in cases where the tenant is responsible for the damage. Upload the invoice and any proof of work — photos, completion reports — and confirm whether any keys were used and have been returned.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: "Quote approved" outcome selected]

The second outcome is that the provider attended to quote rather than repair. If the quote has been approved by the landlord, upload the quote document, and the job will loop back to the scheduling stage so you can book the actual repair visit.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: "Request additional quote" outcome selected, showing provider selection and upload fields]

The third outcome is that the landlord wants a second opinion. Select a different provider, upload the first quote for reference, and add a comment explaining that a comparison quote is needed. The job will go through the scheduling process again with the new provider.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Job Chat section open, showing the different communication options — notes, SMS, email, call]

Before we move on, let us talk about the Chat function in more detail. Chat is not just for landlord communication. You can leave internal notes that only your team can see. You can send an SMS directly to a tenant or provider. You can send an email. And you can make a call, which is logged in the system.

[TIP] This is critically important — every single action, decision, and communication must be documented in the job chat. If it is not in the chat, it did not happen. This is your audit trail, and it protects both you and the company. Make it a habit to log everything, even brief phone calls. A one-line note saying "Called tenant, confirmed access for Thursday 2pm" takes five seconds and could save you hours of dispute resolution later.

That covers the full maintenance workflow, from creating the job all the way through to completion and invoicing.

## Script 5: Compliance — Creating a Compliance Task

**Estimated Duration:** 5–6 minutes

---

[SCREEN: Property Management tab with compliance jobs visible]

Now let us move on to compliance. Compliance tasks are about making sure every property has valid, up-to-date certificates — gas safety, electrical safety, EPC, fire risk assessments, and more. Getting this right is not just good practice, it is a legal requirement.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: A compliance job that has appeared automatically in the queue]

Like maintenance, compliance jobs can be created in two ways. The first is automatic. The system tracks the expiry date of every certificate. Thirty days before a certificate is due to expire, a compliance job is created automatically and assigned to the Property Manager for that property. This gives you a full month to arrange the renewal.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Property Management tab — click Add Request — select Compliance]

The second way is manual creation. Go to Property Management, click Add Request, and select Compliance. Enter the property address, then choose the compliance type — gas safety, EICR, EPC, or whichever certificate is needed. Click Create Job.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: A compliance job open at Step 2 — landlord consent stage, Job Chat visible]

Step two is obtaining landlord consent. Use the Job Chat to contact the landlord, explain which certificate is due for renewal, and ask them to confirm they are happy for us to proceed with our provider.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Calendar or deadline indicator showing the two-week threshold]

Now, here is an important rule. If the landlord does not respond and we are within two weeks of the certificate expiring, you proceed anyway. Compliance is non-negotiable. We cannot allow a property to operate with an expired certificate. Contact the landlord one final time to inform them, then move ahead with scheduling.

[TIP] Never let a certificate expire because a landlord has not responded. Two weeks before expiry is your cut-off. After that, you act regardless.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: A compliance job where the landlord uses their own provider — showing the Assign Progress button and "Certificate Issued" option]

Sometimes a landlord uses their own provider for compliance work. When this happens, the process is different. First, do not select a provider in the system. Instead, use Assign Progress and select Certificate Issued. This tells the system that the certificate is being handled externally.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Job Chat — message to landlord requesting the certificate, then On Hold button]

Next, contact the landlord through Job Chat and ask them to send you the certificate once their provider has completed the work. Then set the job to On Hold. This keeps the job active but pauses automated chasing while you wait.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Certificate upload screen, with "LL pays directly" payment option selected]

Once the landlord sends you the certificate, upload it to the job. For the payment question, select "Landlord pays directly," since they arranged and paid for the work themselves. Once the certificate is uploaded, Dwelly automatically serves a copy to both the tenant and the landlord for their records.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: A compliance job for an EPC where the landlord has declined renewal]

There is one special case to be aware of — EPC renewals. An Energy Performance Certificate is valid for ten years. If a landlord contacts you to say their EPC does not need renewing because it is still valid, check the dates. If the existing EPC is indeed still within its ten-year validity period, you can mark the job as "Not needed." The TC will then close the job. Do not push for an unnecessary renewal.

That covers creating compliance tasks and handling landlord consent. In the next section, we will walk through how to action the compliance task itself.

## Script 6: Compliance — Actioning the Compliance Task

**Estimated Duration:** 7–8 minutes

---

[SCREEN: A compliance job at Step 3 — scheduling stage, provider selection visible]

We are now going to action the compliance task. The landlord has given consent, and it is time to get the certificate renewed.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Provider selection dropdown — highlighting the "preferred" label on a provider]

Step three is scheduling. When assigning a provider, look for the one marked as preferred. Before you confirm, also check the previous certificate for that property — it will often show which provider did the last renewal. Using the same provider where possible means they already know the property, which tends to make the process smoother. Once selected, switch the job to Auto mode. The system will contact the provider and begin the scheduling process.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Job at Step 4 — visit date confirmation, same interface as maintenance]

Step four is visit date confirmation. This works exactly the same way as maintenance. The provider confirms a date via transcription from a masked call, by email reply, or through manual entry by you. Once confirmed, the system sends confirmations and a twenty-four-hour SMS reminder to the tenant automatically.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Job at Step 5 — certificate and invoice upload stage]

Step five is where you upload the certificate and the invoice. After the provider has attended, they should send you the completed certificate and their invoice.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: A notification or flag showing the 48-hour auto-chase]

If the provider has not sent the certificate within forty-eight hours of the visit, the system will automatically chase them. You do not need to follow up manually during this window — let Auto mode do its job. If they still have not responded after the chase, then you step in.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Certificate upload fields — checking the certificate details before uploading]

When the certificate arrives, do not just upload it blindly. Check it. Confirm the property address matches, the date is correct, and the certificate is valid. Then upload both the certificate and the invoice.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: An EICR certificate showing C1 or C2 items flagged]

Now let us talk about what happens when a certificate comes back unsatisfactory. This is where compliance gets more involved, so pay close attention.

For an EICR — an Electrical Installation Condition Report — the certificate may flag C1 or C2 items. C1 means there is an immediate danger. C2 means there is a potential danger. Both require remedial works, and you have twenty-eight days to address them.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: A Gas Safety Certificate flagged as unsafe]

For Gas Safety, if the certificate comes back as unsafe, the gas supply may need to be isolated, repairs carried out, and a new certificate issued after the work is done.

Other certificates with possible remedial outcomes include fire alarm certificates, PAT testing, EPC reports, and fire risk assessments. Each has its own remediation requirements, but the process you follow in the portal is the same.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: File Manager tab on the property — NOT the job — uploading the unsatisfactory certificate]

Here is a crucial step. When you receive an unsatisfactory certificate, upload it through the property's File Manager tab, not through the job itself. Go to the property, open File Manager, and upload the document there. This keeps the original certificate on the property record for compliance tracking.

[TIP] Always upload unsatisfactory certificates via File Manager on the property, never via the job. The job is for the satisfactory final certificate only.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: The job — clicking On Hold, entering "remedial works required" as the reason]

After uploading the unsatisfactory certificate to File Manager, go back to the compliance job and set it to On Hold. In the reason field, enter "remedial works required." This pauses the compliance job while the remedial work is carried out.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Property Management — Add Request — Maintenance Request — creating a new job for the remedial works]

Next, create a new maintenance request for the remedial works. Go to Property Management, Add Request, Maintenance Request, and create a job specifically for the repairs identified in the unsatisfactory certificate. This maintenance job follows the standard maintenance workflow — landlord consent, provider scheduling, visit, completion.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: The original compliance job — resuming it after remedials are complete]

Once the remedial works are finished, go back to the original compliance job. If the provider needs to re-attend to issue a new satisfactory certificate, schedule that through the existing compliance job using the normal process. When the satisfactory certificate arrives, upload it to the job.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Property Licence compliance task open — showing manual workflow elements]

There is one compliance type that works differently — Property Licences. Licence tasks follow a manual flow. There are no automated emails or chasing sequences. You will need to handle all coordination yourself — contacting the council, gathering documents, submitting applications, and following up on progress. Keep detailed notes in the Job Chat throughout.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Job at Step 6 — completion stage, showing auto-sent certificate confirmations]

Step six is completion. Once a valid certificate and invoice have been uploaded, the system handles the rest. The certificate is automatically sent to both the tenant and the landlord. The contractor payment is confirmed. And the job is marked as completed.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: A job flagged with MP_TIME_FORCE — showing the urgency indicator]

Before we finish, let us cover two corner cases. The first is MP_TIME_FORCE. This flag appears when a certificate is less than fourteen days from expiry. It signals extreme urgency. When you see this, drop everything else and prioritise that job. An expired certificate is a compliance breach, and it puts both the landlord and the company at risk.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: A compliance job for a vacant or untenanted property]

The second corner case is vacant properties. Even if a property has no tenant, compliance obligations still apply. Gas safety, electrical safety, and other certificates must remain valid regardless of occupancy. The process is the same, but you may need to coordinate access differently since there is no tenant to let the provider in. Check with the landlord about key access or arrange to meet the provider at the property yourself.

[TIP] Vacant does not mean exempt. Every managed property must have valid certificates at all times, whether or not anyone is living there.

That covers the full compliance workflow. You now know how to create compliance tasks, handle landlord consent, manage providers, deal with unsatisfactory certificates and remedial works, and close out completed jobs.
