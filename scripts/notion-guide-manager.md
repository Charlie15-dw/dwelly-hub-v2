# Manager's Guide to the Dwelly Training Portal

This guide is for managers who need to track, support and sign off their team's training on the Dwelly Training Portal. It covers everything from reading the dashboard to signing off competencies and handling staff who are struggling.

---

## What Is the Dwelly Training Portal?

The Dwelly Training Portal is the central hub for all property management training. It brings together video learning, knowledge resources, formal assessments and competency tracking in one place.

As a manager, your role in the portal is not just to observe — it is to actively verify that your team members have reached the standard required to work independently. The portal gives you the tools to do that: a live view of every team member's progress, a complete record of their assessment results (including every individual answer), and a formal sign-off mechanism for the highest-risk areas of the role.

### How It Fits Into the Training Programme

The training programme is structured across three phases. The portal covers all of them:

- **Phase 1 — Induction:** Staff complete their Learning Profile and Training Guide. This sets the foundation and contextualises their learning path.
- **Phase 2 — Core Learning:** Staff work through the Video Modules, the Knowledge Base, and all formal assessments — including the Master Assessment.
- **Phase 3 — Sign-Off and Ongoing Learning:** You sign off competencies in the Competency Passport. Staff continue with monthly Recap & Refresh quizzes to keep their knowledge current.

The recommended journey for all new staff is:

1. Learning Profile
2. Training Guide
3. Video Modules
4. Knowledge Base
5. Assessments
6. Competency Passport

Staff should not be considered fully trained until they have passed the Master Assessment and had their sign-off gate competencies formally approved by you.

## The Manager Dashboard

### What It Shows You

The Manager Dashboard is your operational view of your entire team's training status. When you log in and navigate to the Manager Dashboard, you will see a table-style overview with one row per team member.

For each person, the dashboard shows:

- **Name and role**
- **Modules completed** — how many assessments they have passed out of 15
- **Assessment score** — their average score across all assessments attempted
- **Last active date** — when they last logged into the portal
- **Overall progress** — a percentage and progress bar showing how far through the programme they are
- **Status** — a summary label (Not started, In progress, Completed)

### Viewing a Team Member's Full Detail

Click any team member's name in the table to open their individual detail view. This shows you a complete breakdown of every assessment they have attempted, including:

- Every question asked in each assessment
- The answer they gave
- Whether it was correct
- The correct answer (for any they got wrong)
- The rationale explaining why the correct answer is right
- All previous attempts (not just the most recent), clearly labelled

This gives you a full picture of where their knowledge is strong and where gaps exist — not just whether they passed or failed.

### How to Use It Day-to-Day

The dashboard is designed to be scanned quickly. Use it to:

- Identify anyone who has not logged in recently — the Last Active date will flag staff who have gone quiet
- Spot anyone stuck mid-programme who may need a nudge or support
- Monitor assessment scores across the team and identify anyone who may need extra support
- Check team-wide progress ahead of one-to-ones or training reviews

Make it a habit to review the dashboard at least once a week during the onboarding period for any new staff member, and at least monthly for your established team.

## Tracking Individual Team Member Progress

### Drilling Down on a Team Member

From the Manager Dashboard, click on any team member's name to open their individual view. This shows you a full breakdown of:

- Every assessment attempted, with scores and dates
- All individual answers per attempt — correct and incorrect — with rationale
- Assessments not yet attempted (listed at the bottom)
- Overall pass rate and average score in the header

### What to Look For

When reviewing an individual's progress, pay attention to:

- **Gaps in assessment completion** — if someone has passed several modules but not others, check in with them. It may be a workload issue or a confidence issue.
- **Low assessment scores** — a pass is 80%. Scores between 80% and 85% on the first attempt are worth noting, even if they passed. They may indicate shaky knowledge in specific areas.
- **Stalled progress** — if their last active date is more than a week ago and they haven't finished their training, follow up directly.
- **Patterns in incorrect answers** — the detail view shows exactly which questions they got wrong across every attempt. Look for recurring errors in the same topic area.
- **Incomplete Competency Passport** — if assessments are done but the passport has outstanding competencies, that is a prompt for you to act, not for them.

## The Competency Passport

### What It Is

Every staff member has their own Competency Passport — a structured record of the skills and knowledge areas they need to demonstrate for their role. It is the formal record of what they can do, not just what they have studied.

The passport contains:

- **Competency Sign-Off Gates** — specific high-risk areas that require your formal sign-off
- **Assessment progress** — a live count of assessments passed out of 15
- **T&C Supervision tracker** — key milestones in their 90-day supervised period
- **Annual Re-Certification** — a reminder of when their compliance re-cert is due
- **Achievement Badges** — earned as they reach milestones throughout the programme

### How to Sign Off Competencies

You are responsible for signing off competencies. This is a deliberate act — it means you have reviewed the evidence and are satisfied the staff member has met the required standard.

To sign off a competency:

1. Open the staff member's Competency Passport from their individual view
2. Review the competency in question — check their assessment results, any observed work or conversations that support their readiness
3. If you are satisfied, click to sign off and confirm
4. The date of sign-off is recorded automatically

Do not sign off competencies as a formality. The Competency Passport is a legal and operational record. If a compliance issue arises, the sign-off log will be part of any investigation into what training the staff member had received.

## Sign-Off Gates

### What They Are

Sign-off gates are specific competency areas that are designated as high risk. A staff member cannot be considered ready to work independently in these areas until you have formally signed them off.

The four sign-off gates in the portal are:

- **Gas Safety Obligations** — legal responsibilities around gas safety certificates, landlord duties and CP12 records
- **Deposit Protection** — correct handling of tenancy deposits under the relevant government-backed schemes
- **Right to Rent Compliance** — conducting and recording right to rent checks in line with Home Office requirements
- **HMO Management** — houses in multiple occupation, including licensing thresholds, management regulations and fire safety obligations

### Why They Matter

These four areas carry significant legal and financial risk for your business. Errors in any of them can result in:

- Criminal prosecution or civil penalties for the company
- Fines running into thousands of pounds (deposit protection failures and right to rent breaches each carry statutory penalties)
- Licensing enforcement action for unlicensed HMOs
- Serious harm to tenants in the case of gas safety failures

Because of this, you must not allow a staff member to handle these areas independently until you are genuinely confident in their understanding — not just because they have passed the relevant assessment.

### How to Approach Sign-Off Gate Reviews

Before signing off a gate competency, consider:

- **Have they passed the relevant section assessment with a strong score?** A pass is 80%, but for gate competencies, look for scores in the 90%+ range as a marker of genuine confidence.
- **Have they demonstrated understanding in practice?** Where possible, shadow them on a relevant task or discuss a scenario before signing off.
- **Have they asked sensible questions?** Staff who engage with the material actively — asking about edge cases, flagging uncertainties — are often better prepared than those who move through quietly.
- **Do they know when to escalate?** Especially for HMO and deposit protection, they should be able to articulate when something requires your input or external advice.

If you have doubts, delay the sign-off, direct them back to the relevant module or knowledge base section, and revisit.

## Understanding Assessment Results

### The Assessment Structure

There are **15 assessments in total** across the programme:

| Assessment Type | Count | Pass Mark | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Video Module Assessments | 10 | 80% | 2 per section across Maintenance, Compliance, Inspections, Move-Outs and Support |
| Knowledge Base Assessments | 3 | 80% | Difficult Conversations, Handling Disrepair Claims, Understanding a P&L |
| Full Programme Assessment | 1 | 80% | Covers all five video module sections combined |
| Master Assessment | 1 | 80% | Scenario-based, covers everything across the whole programme |

The Master Assessment is the most important. It is scenario-based, meaning it tests applied judgement, not just recall. A staff member who passes the Master Assessment has demonstrated they can think through real-world situations, not just reproduce facts.

### Reading the Results

Assessment scores are visible in the individual staff member view. For each assessment, you can see:

- The score achieved and whether it was a pass
- Whether it was a first attempt or a re-sit, with all previous attempts shown
- The date of completion
- Every question, the answer given, and whether it was correct — including the correct answer and rationale for any they got wrong

### What to Do If Someone Fails

If a staff member fails an assessment, do not treat it as a minor administrative issue. A failure below 80% means there are gaps in their knowledge that could translate into errors on the job.

**Immediate steps:**

1. **Do not allow them to re-sit immediately without preparation.** Going back in without addressing the gaps is likely to produce the same result.
2. **Review their answer detail in the member view.** The portal shows you exactly which questions they got wrong and why the correct answer is right — use this to target your support.
3. **Have a conversation.** Ask them what they found difficult. Is it a knowledge gap, a confidence issue or something else? The answer shapes your support.
4. **Set a re-sit date** after they have had time to revisit the material — typically a week or more, depending on the assessment.

**If someone fails the Master Assessment:**

This is a significant flag. The Master Assessment covers everything, and failure means there are substantial gaps. Do not progress them into unsupported work in regulated areas. Consider:

- Going back through specific modules rather than the whole programme
- More structured oversight of their work while they re-prepare
- A formal check-in to understand whether the role is the right fit

**If someone fails the same assessment multiple times:**

Escalate your concern. This goes beyond a training issue and may require a formal performance conversation. Document your interventions and keep a record of each attempt and what support was provided.

## Recap & Refresh

### What It Is

The Recap & Refresh section contains monthly quizzes available under the Property Managers section of the portal. These are shorter, lower-stakes quizzes designed to keep knowledge fresh after the main training is complete.

### Why It Matters

Property management law and best practice changes. Legislation is updated, case law shifts, and what was correct procedure two years ago may not be today. Recap & Refresh is how your team stays current rather than operating on outdated knowledge.

It is also a useful early warning system. If someone consistently scores poorly on a monthly quiz topic, it may indicate a knowledge area that needs refreshing before it causes a problem in their day-to-day work.

### How to Encourage Completion

- **Make it a team norm, not an individual task.** If completing the monthly quiz is just part of how your team operates, uptake will be much higher than if it is left to individual motivation.
- **Reference it in one-to-ones.** A quick mention of "have you done this month's refresh?" signals that you are paying attention and that it matters.
- **Acknowledge strong scores.** A brief positive comment in a team message when someone does well costs you nothing and reinforces the behaviour.
- **Monitor it on the dashboard.** Last active dates and completion records will show you who is engaging. If someone has not touched Recap & Refresh in several months, raise it directly.

## Reporting and Evidence

### What the Portal Records

The portal maintains a running record that serves as your primary evidence of training compliance. For each staff member, you have access to:

- **Assessment history** — all attempts, scores and dates for every assessment type, including full answer detail
- **Competency Passport** — the full sign-off record including dates and the approving manager
- **T&C Supervision tracker** — milestones in the supervised working period
- **Last active dates** — a timestamped record of portal engagement

### Using Records for Compliance and Audits

If your business is inspected by a letting agent redress scheme, a licensing authority or any other body, training records may be requested. The portal provides a clear, datestamped audit trail.

You should:

- Ensure sign-offs are completed promptly and accurately — not backdated
- Use the competency passport as the primary record of an individual's training status, not informal memory or notes
- Keep your own note of any additional mentoring or shadowing that falls outside the portal — this supplements the portal record

### Before Granting Independent Working Status

Before a staff member moves into fully independent operation, check that:

- Learning Profile is completed
- Training Guide has been worked through
- All 10 video module assessments are passed (80%+)
- Full programme assessment is passed
- All three knowledge base assessments are passed
- Master Assessment is passed
- All four sign-off gate competencies are formally approved in their Competency Passport

Do not advance someone to independent status based on time served or seniority. The portal record is the standard.

## What to Do If a Staff Member Is Struggling

### Identifying Struggling Staff

The portal gives you early signals. Watch for:

- **Stalled progress** — not completing modules at a reasonable pace
- **Low assessment scores** on first attempts, even if eventually passing
- **Multiple failed attempts** on the same assessment
- **Low engagement** — last active dates showing long gaps
- **Incomplete Competency Passport** — assessments done but no sign-off request, which can suggest lack of confidence

### How to Respond

The right response depends on what is driving the difficulty.

**If it is a knowledge gap:**
Use the member detail view to identify exactly which questions they are getting wrong and in which topic area. Direct them back to the specific module or knowledge base section. Do not have them re-do everything — identify the weak area and target it. Set a re-sit date and check in between now and then.

**If it is a confidence issue:**
Some staff find formal assessments stressful even when they know the material well. Spend time talking through the content with them in an informal setting. Reassurance and conversation can be as useful as additional study time.

**If it is a workload issue:**
If they are struggling to find time to train because of operational pressures, that is a management problem, not a training problem. Review their workload and protect time for training during the onboarding period. Training that keeps getting pushed back will eventually not happen at all.

**If it is a comprehension or literacy issue:**
Some staff may struggle with written assessments or dense technical content. Consider supplementing portal learning with verbal discussion, walkthroughs or practical shadowing. Speak to HR or a learning and development contact if you believe additional support may be needed.

**If they are repeatedly failing and support is not working:**
This requires a formal process. Document every intervention you have made, every re-sit and every support conversation. If the role requires competencies that the staff member cannot reach, that is a performance and suitability conversation that goes beyond the training portal.

### Escalation

If you are concerned about a staff member's competence in a regulated or high-risk area, do not wait for a training milestone to flag it. Raise it with your line manager or HR contact. The training portal provides the evidence; your judgement provides the context.
