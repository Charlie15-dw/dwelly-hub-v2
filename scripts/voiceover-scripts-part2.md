## Script 7: Move-Outs — Initiating the Move-Out Process

**Estimated Duration:** 5–6 minutes

---

[SCREEN: Dwelly dashboard — Property Management section visible]

Welcome back. In this video we are going to walk through the first half of the move-out process in Dwelly. Move-outs involve several careful steps, so we have split this into two parts. This video covers everything from initiating the request through to scheduling the checkout inventory.

[PAUSE]

Let's start with how move-out requests are created. There are two ways this can happen. The first is automatic — when a tenant submits a move-out request through the tenant portal, Dwelly creates the job for you. The second is manual, which is what we will walk through now.

[SCREEN: Property Management → Add Request → Move-Out Request]

To create a move-out request manually, navigate to Property Management, then click Add Request, and select Move-Out Request. You will be prompted to enter the property address. Start typing and select the correct property from the dropdown.

[PAUSE]

Next, enter the proposed move-out date and add a description — this is where you note any relevant details, such as the reason for the move-out or any context from the tenant. Once everything looks correct, click Create Job.

[PAUSE]

[TIP] If there are two or more tenants on the tenancy, you must have written confirmation from ALL tenants before proceeding. Do not move forward based on a request from just one tenant — this is a legal safeguard.

[SCREEN: Move-out job created — Step 2 visible]

Now we move to Step 2, which is confirming that the tenant is actually allowed to move out. There are a few things to verify here.

First, check whether the tenant has given the correct notice period. This depends on the terms of their tenancy agreement. If the notice period has not been met, you will need to communicate this back to the tenant before anything can proceed.

Second, look for any special tenancy conditions. Some tenancies have break clauses, fixed-end dates, or other stipulations that affect whether the tenant can leave at the proposed time.

[TIP] Keep the move-out date up to date at all times. The Lettings Negotiators rely on this date to begin the re-letting process, so an inaccurate date can cause significant delays in finding new tenants for the property.

One more important point here. Confirming the move-out date does not pause invoice generation. That is a completely separate action. If you need to stop invoices from being generated, you must do that independently — we will cover that in Part 2.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Step 3 — Schedule checkout inventory]

Step 3 is scheduling the checkout inventory. How you do this depends on whether the inspection will be carried out by an internal employee or an external contractor.

If you are booking an internal employee — one of our Property Specialists — Dwelly will display their Google Calendar directly in the system. Browse the available time slots, select one that works, and confirm the booking.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Calendar view with available slots]

Select the slot, and Dwelly handles the rest — a calendar event is created and the tenant receives a notification.

Now, if you are using an external contractor instead, their calendar will not appear in Dwelly. In that case, contact the contractor directly to agree on a date and time, then come back to Dwelly and enter that date manually.

[PAUSE]

And that covers the first half of the move-out process. You now know how to create the request, confirm the tenant is eligible to leave, and schedule the checkout inventory. In the next video, we will pick up from the checkout inspection itself and work through to closing the job entirely.

## Script 8: Move-Outs — Completing the Move-Out

**Estimated Duration:** 7–8 minutes

---

[SCREEN: Move-out job — Step 4 visible]

Welcome to Part 2 of the move-out process. In the previous video we covered initiating the move-out and scheduling the checkout inventory. Now we are going to work through all the remaining steps — from the checkout inspection right through to closing the job permanently.

[PAUSE]

We pick up at Step 4, which is processing the checkout inspection. Once the inspection has taken place, you need to obtain the checkout report. If it was done by an internal Property Specialist, they should upload it directly. If it was done by an external contractor, chase them for it and upload it yourself.

[SCREEN: Upload section for checkout report]

Upload the report into the system. Once it is in, you will want to compare it with the original check-in report. This comparison is what drives the decisions in the next few steps.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Step 5 — Meter readings entry]

Step 5 is entering the meter readings. You will see fields for Water, Gas, and Electricity. Enter the final readings from the checkout inspection here.

[TIP] If a meter is missing from this screen, it means it has not been set up for the property. To fix this, go to Properties, then Maintenance, then Property Meters, and add the missing meter there. Then come back and enter the reading.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Step 6 — Checkout report review with colour indicators]

Step 6 is reviewing the checkout report. Dwelly uses a traffic light system to make this straightforward.

Green means the property is clean and in good condition — the deposit can go back to the tenant in full. Yellow means the property needs some cleaning or minor attention. Red means there is damage or repairs are needed, and costs will likely be deducted from the deposit.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Step 7 — Maintenance tasks and payment allocation]

Step 7 is where you handle any maintenance tasks that arise from the inspection. The key decision here is whether an issue is tenant damage or general landlord responsibility.

You will see a payment allocation section with two important fields: Total Quote, which is the full cost of the work, and Owed by Tenant, which is the portion the tenant is responsible for.

Here is how to think about the split. Normal wear and tear is the landlord's responsibility — that is just the natural ageing of the property. Tenant damage is the tenant's responsibility — anything beyond reasonable wear and tear. Cleaning costs are also typically allocated to the tenant if the property was not left in the condition it was found.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Step 8 — Security deposit section]

Step 8 is the security deposit. The good news here is that Dwelly auto-calculates the deposit return based on all the information you have entered in the previous steps.

Once the calculation is ready, you need to send two communications. First, send the landlord an explanation that includes the cost breakdown and a clear indication of what the tenant is responsible for. Second, tell the tenant what was found during the inspection, what costs are being deducted, and remind them of the 14-day dispute window.

[TIP] The 14-day dispute window is a legal requirement. Make sure you communicate this clearly to the tenant so they understand their rights.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Step 9 — Three closing buttons visible: blue, yellow, red]

Step 9 is the closing stage, and this is where a lot of people get confused. There are three buttons, and each one serves a different purpose.

The blue button means the property is ready. This confirms the physical condition of the property — it is clean, repaired, and suitable for the next tenant.

The yellow button means mediation is finished. This confirms the financial side — all disputes are resolved and the deposit allocation is agreed.

The red button means tenancy ended. This is the big one. Clicking this stops invoice generation. Press it promptly once the tenancy has officially ended — if you delay, invoices will continue to be generated incorrectly.

[TIP] A job is only considered fully completed when three conditions are met: the Tenancy Ended button has been clicked, the checkout report has been uploaded, and a result has been selected. All three must be done.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Step 10 — Key check section]

Finally, Step 10 is the key check. Confirm that all keys have been returned by the tenant. Once verified, click "Everything is ready." This permanently closes the job — there is no going back after this point, so make sure everything is genuinely complete before you click.

[PAUSE]

And that is the full move-out process from start to finish. To recap: process the inspection, enter meter readings, review the report, handle maintenance, sort the deposit, close the job in the correct order, and confirm the keys. Take your time with each step — accuracy here prevents problems down the line.

## Script 9: Inspections — Creating an Inspection Task

**Estimated Duration:** 4–5 minutes

---

[SCREEN: Dwelly dashboard — Inspections section]

In this video we are going to cover how inspection tasks work in Dwelly. This is Part 1, focusing on how inspections are created and how to schedule the visit.

Let's start with a very important point.

[TIP] Inspections are never created manually. They are always auto-generated by the system. You do not need to go in and create an inspection job yourself — Dwelly handles this for you.

[PAUSE]

So how does Dwelly know when to create one? It is based on two things: the date of the most recent inspection and the frequency set by the managing agency.

[SCREEN: Example of an inspection job with agency label]

The frequencies differ by agency. For Lime properties, the first inspection is at 3 months, then every 6 months after that. For GR properties, the first inspection is at 1 month, then every 4 months. And for Pennington properties, it is 3 months for the first, then every 6 months — the same pattern as Lime.

Dwelly creates the inspection job 14 days before the due date. When it appears in your queue, it will be labelled as an Inspection Request.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Step 2 — Inspector Booking, three-step bar: Pick Date & Time / Your Details / Confirm]

Now, Step 2 is booking the visit. This now runs through the Inspector Booking flow, which has three steps across the top: Pick Date and Time, Your Details, and Confirm.

[SCREEN: Pick a Date & Time — calendar with Property Specialist toggles and availability bands]

You start on Pick a Date and Time. Rather than a single person's calendar, you now see all of your Property Specialists in one view. Each Property Specialist has a toggle at the top — for example John and Geoff — and a pill shows how many are active, such as "1 of 2 active". Switch Property Specialists on or off to compare availability, and each day shows that Property Specialist's available window, for example "Geoff: available 9 to 5". Use the week arrows to move between weeks.

[TIP] Dwelly still does not have a routing system. You need to manually check postcodes to make sure the Property Specialist you are booking is actually going to be in the right area on that day. Having everyone in one calendar helps — pick whoever is already going to be nearby rather than just the first free slot.

[PAUSE]

Click any free slot to select it. It highlights, and a bar at the bottom summarises your choice — for example "Geoff — Monday 23 August at 10:00". When you are happy, click Continue.

[SCREEN: Step 2 — Your Details form, Your Name and Property Address marked required]

The second step is Your Details, and this step must be completed before you can go any further. Two fields carry a red asterisk and are mandatory — Your Name and the Property Address — and the booking will not submit without them. The rest are optional but help the Property Specialist: Your Email, a Job Reference, the Tenant Name and Phone Number, where the Keys are, the Duration, and a Notes box for anything like a key safe code or the tenant preferring afternoon visits. Job Type is pre-filled as Inspection and Duration defaults to 15 minutes, so only change that if the visit needs longer.

[PAUSE]

When the details are in, click Review Booking — or Back if you need to change the slot. The final step, Confirm, shows the visit time and a summary so you can check everything before it is booked.

If the inspection is being done by an external party, their availability will not be visible in Dwelly. Contact them directly to agree a date and time, then come back, select the matching slot, and complete Your Details in the same way.

[SCREEN: Confirmation — calendar event created]

Once the booking is confirmed — whether internal or external — a calendar event is automatically created. The tenant receives a confirmation message and will also get a reminder SMS 24 hours before the visit.

[PAUSE]

Now, what happens if you need to change the date? To reschedule, click Pause, then go to Assign Progress, select Change Inspection Time, and enter the new date. The system will update the calendar event and notify the tenant.

What about cancellations? You cannot cancel an inspection yourself. That permission sits with the Team Coordinator. If an inspection needs to be cancelled, speak to your Team Coordinator and they will handle it.

[PAUSE]

That covers the creation and scheduling side of inspections. In the next video, we will walk through what happens after the visit — uploading the report and processing the results.

## Script 10: Inspections — Completing the Inspection

**Estimated Duration:** 4–5 minutes

---

[SCREEN: Inspection job — post-visit stage]

Welcome to Part 2 of inspections. The visit has taken place, and now it is time to process the results. Let's walk through the remaining steps.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Step 3 — Upload inspection file section, chatbot message visible]

Step 3 is uploading the inspection file. After the visit, the system automatically sends a message through the chatbot asking the Property Specialist for the inspection results. Keep an eye on this — if you have not received the report within a reasonable timeframe, chase the Property Specialist directly.

Once you have the report, upload the document into Dwelly. You will also need to enter the inspection date and the expiry date. Then input the current state of the property and note any issues that were found.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Step 4 — Process report, Yes/No options visible]

Step 4 is processing the report based on the recommendations. There are two paths here.

If no issues were found, this is straightforward. Press "No" when asked if there are issues, then click Submit. The job closes automatically, and the landlord is informed with a copy of the report. Clean and simple.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: "Yes" selected — Comment for LL field visible]

If issues were found, press "Yes." You will see a field called "Comment for LL" — this is where you describe the current state of the property and what needs attention. Write a clear, professional summary. Once you submit, this comment is automatically sent to the landlord along with the report.

[TIP] If something needs to be fixed — a repair, a replacement, anything that requires action — do not try to handle it within the inspection job itself. Instead, create a separate manual maintenance job for each individual issue. The inspection job is for recording and reporting. Repairs are tracked independently as their own maintenance tasks.

[PAUSE]

This is a common mistake, so it is worth repeating. The inspection job is for documentation and landlord communication. Any physical work that needs doing gets its own maintenance job. Keeping these separate ensures proper tracking, proper invoicing, and a clear audit trail.

[PAUSE]

And that is inspections complete. You now know the full cycle — from the auto-generated job appearing in your queue, through scheduling the visit, uploading the report, and processing the outcome. The key things to remember are: never create inspections manually, check geography when booking internal specialists, and always create separate maintenance jobs for any repairs.

## Script 11: Support — Raising a Support Task

**Estimated Duration:** 3–4 minutes

---

[SCREEN: Dwelly dashboard — Support section]

In this video we are going to cover support tasks. These are a bit different from the other job types you have seen so far, so pay close attention to how they work.

Support requests happen when someone contacts the agency about something that falls outside of an active job. This could be a follow-up question after a completed maintenance job, a former tenant calling with a query, or just a general question from a landlord or tenant.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Support job appearing in queue]

When this happens, Dwelly creates a Support job automatically. And here is something critical to understand.

[TIP] Support tickets cannot be created manually. They are only generated when a third party contacts the agency through the system. If someone contacts you outside of Dwelly — say through a personal email or a direct phone call — that interaction must be handled outside the system. You cannot log it as a support task in Dwelly.

[PAUSE]

Now, support jobs are fundamentally different from other job types because they are always created in Manual mode. What does that mean? It means Dwelly will not automate any messages. Dwelly will not move the job forward through stages. You handle the entire thing from start to finish, yourself.

There are no automated tenant notifications, no automated landlord updates, no system-driven progress. It is entirely on you.

[SCREEN: Chat section within a support job]

Your job is to resolve the issue. That might mean answering a question, providing information, coordinating with another team, or solving a specific problem. Whatever the request is, handle it and make sure you document everything in the Chat section. This is important for handovers and for maintaining a clear record of what was discussed and resolved.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Assign Progress → Complete Support Task]

When the issue is resolved, closing the job is simple. Go to Assign Progress, select Complete Support Task, and the job moves to the Completed column.

[PAUSE]

That is support tasks in a nutshell. Remember — they cannot be created manually, they run in Manual mode with no automations, and you are responsible for the entire lifecycle. Document everything in Chat, resolve the issue, and close it out.

## Script 12: Right to Rent Checks

**Estimated Duration:** 4–5 minutes

---

[SCREEN: Dwelly dashboard — Right to Rent section]

In this video we are covering Right to Rent checks. This is a legal requirement in England — every tenant must have permission to live in the United Kingdom, and it is our responsibility to verify this.

Now, the initial Right to Rent check is done by the Tenant Find team when the tenancy begins. However, some tenants have time-limited immigration status, which means their right to rent expires and needs to be rechecked during the tenancy. That is where you come in.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Right to Rent job auto-created, timeline visible]

Step 1. Dwelly creates the job automatically, two months before the current Right to Rent status expires. This gives you plenty of time to complete the check before the deadline.

Once the job is created, Dwelly automatically emails the tenant requesting their share code and date of birth. If the tenant does not respond, the system sends a reminder after 96 hours, and then waits for one week.

[TIP] If the tenant has not responded within 7 days, the job switches to Manual mode. At that point, the automations stop and it is your responsibility to follow up directly — by phone, SMS, or email. Do not let this sit. Right to Rent compliance is a legal obligation, and delays can have serious consequences.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Step 2 — RTR Verification section with share code and DOB fields]

Step 2 is the actual verification. Once you have the tenant's share code and date of birth, enter them into the verification section in Dwelly. Then click Send RTR Request. This puts the job back into Auto mode.

If the share code is valid, the system confirms the check and the job is completed automatically. If the share code is invalid, the job returns to your queue for follow-up. You will need to contact the tenant again to get the correct details.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Step 3 — Visual likeness verification]

Step 3 is the visual likeness verification. This requires a video call with the tenant to verify their identity. During the call, check their physical likeness against their documentation, confirm their name, and confirm their date of birth.

If everything matches, you can close the job. If there is no match — if something does not add up — do not close the job yourself. Escalate it immediately to your Team Coordinator.

[TIP] Closing or cancelling Right to Rent tasks is restricted to the Supervisor role. If you need a task closed or cancelled and do not have the permission, reach out to Product Support for assistance.

[PAUSE]

That covers the full Right to Rent process. The key points to remember are: jobs are created automatically two months before expiry, the system handles initial contact with the tenant, but if they do not respond within a week you must step in manually. Always verify identity through a video call, and escalate any mismatches rather than making judgement calls on your own.

## Script 13: Onboarding a New Provider

**Estimated Duration:** 5–6 minutes

---

[SCREEN: Dwelly dashboard — Home page]

In this video we are going to walk through how to add a new service provider to Dwelly. Whether it is a plumber, electrician, cleaner, or any other contractor, this process ensures they are set up correctly so they can be assigned to jobs.

Before we begin — most providers are added automatically during migration when an agency first comes onto the platform. But there will be times when you need to add a new one manually, and that is what this video covers.

[PAUSE]

[TIP] Before you add any provider, check the agency-specific provider onboarding guide. This is provided by Rut Maree and shared on Slack. Each agency may have slightly different requirements or conventions, so start there.

[SCREEN: Home → Property Management → Service Providers]

Step 1 — where to add a provider. From the Home screen, go to Property Management, then Service Providers, and click Add Provider. You can also add a provider directly from inside a job if you are in the middle of assigning work and realise the provider is not in the system yet.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Provider input form with mandatory fields highlighted]

Step 2 is entering the provider's details. There are several mandatory fields you need to complete. Name — the provider or company name. Receive SMS with Email — this defaults to zero, leave it unless told otherwise. Contact name — the individual you deal with. Contact email. Contact phone number. And the Agency ID — make sure this is correct, as it links the provider to the right agency.

Once everything is filled in, click Save. You should see a blue confirmation bar appear at the top of the screen — that means the provider has been created successfully.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Blue confirmation bar with Geo Zones option visible]

Step 3 is setting up Geo Zones. These define the geographic areas the provider covers. If the Agency ID was entered correctly, some of this may already be pre-configured. But you should always check.

Click on Geo Zones in the blue confirmation bar. This opens the coverage mapping tool.

[SCREEN: geojson.io website with map visible]

You will use geojson.io to draw the coverage area. Open the site, find the area the provider covers on the map, and draw a circle or polygon around it. Once you have the shape, copy the generated code.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Back in Dwelly — Geo Zone paste field]

Come back to Dwelly, paste the code into the Geo Zone field, and click Add or Update Geo Zone.

[SCREEN: Provider name selected — service category checkboxes visible]

Now click on the provider's name and you will see a list of service categories — things like plumbing, electrical, cleaning, general maintenance, and so on. Tick all the categories that apply to this provider, then click Save Changes.

[PAUSE]

The provider is now fully set up. They will appear as an option whenever you are selecting a provider for a job that matches their service categories and geographic coverage.

[PAUSE]

To recap: check the onboarding guide first, add the provider through Service Providers or directly from a job, fill in all mandatory fields, set up their Geo Zone coverage, and assign their service categories. Once that is done, they are ready to be used across the platform.

## Script 14: Best Practices and Auto vs Hold

**Estimated Duration:** 6–7 minutes

---

[SCREEN: Dwelly dashboard — overview of active jobs]

This final video covers best practices for working in Dwelly. These are the habits and principles that separate a good property manager from a great one on this platform. Even if you are confident with the system, this is worth watching carefully.

[PAUSE]

Let's start with the most fundamental rule.

[TIP] All communication must go through Dwelly. Never use Outlook, personal email, WhatsApp, or any external channel to communicate with tenants, landlords, or providers about active jobs. Everything must be logged in the system.

Why? Because Dwelly is the single source of truth. If a conversation happens outside the platform, it does not exist as far as the system is concerned. That creates gaps in the record, causes confusion during handovers, and undermines the audit trail.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Example of masked phone number in Dwelly]

On a related note, never share direct contact details. Dwelly uses masked phone numbers to protect everyone's privacy. When a tenant, landlord, or provider needs to call someone, they use the masked number provided by the system. Do not give out personal or direct office numbers.

[PAUSE]

Now let's talk about chasing. When you are waiting for a response from a provider, Dwelly has a built-in reminder cadence. The system sends automatic reminders at 24 hours, then another at 24 hours, and then again at 96 hours. If you still have not heard back after that sequence, it is time to pick up the phone and call the provider directly.

[TIP] The auto-reminders are there to save you time, but they do not replace human follow-up. If a provider is consistently unresponsive after the automated chasing, escalate the issue and consider whether they should remain on your provider list.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Job mode options — Auto, Pause, Manual, Hold]

Now, let's cover one of the most important concepts in Dwelly — the job modes. There are four: Auto, Pause, Manual, and Hold.

Auto is the default and the ideal state for any job. In Auto mode, Dwelly handles reminders, notifications, and stage progression. This is where you want your jobs to be whenever possible.

Pause is for temporary situations. Use it when you need to stop the automations briefly — for example, while you wait for a specific piece of information before the job can continue.

Manual mode turns off all automations entirely. You drive everything yourself. This is used for support tasks and for situations where the standard workflow does not apply.

Hold is for jobs with a future action date that is far away. Use Hold only when you are genuinely waiting for something that will not happen for a while — not as a way to park jobs you have not got round to yet.

[TIP] Auto is the ideal. Hold is only for when you are genuinely waiting on something in the far future. Do not use Hold as a way to hide jobs from your active queue. If a job needs attention, it should be in Auto or Manual — not buried in Hold.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Chat section within a job — notes and messages visible]

Documentation is everything. Use the Chat section in every job to record what is happening, what has been agreed, and what the next steps are. If you are off sick tomorrow and a colleague picks up your jobs, they should be able to read the Chat and know exactly where things stand.

[SCREEN: Notes section — task, property, landlord, tenancy notes]

Beyond Chat, Dwelly has a Notes system with four levels: task notes, property notes, landlord notes, and tenancy notes. Pinned notes are visible across all related jobs, so if there is something important about a property or a landlord that every PM should know, pin it.

[PAUSE]

[SCREEN: Dashboard with job queue]

Finally, let's talk about professionalism and daily habits. Clear your queue every day. Work by urgency — the jobs at the top of your list should be the ones closest to a deadline or with the most pressing issues. Respond promptly to all parties. And keep everyone informed — tenants, landlords, and providers should never have to chase you for an update.

[TIP] A clean queue at the end of the day means nothing has been forgotten. Even if a job cannot be completed today, make sure it has been acknowledged, documented, and scheduled for follow-up.

[PAUSE]

That wraps up not just this video, but the entire training series. You now have a thorough understanding of how Dwelly works across maintenance, move-ins, move-outs, inspections, support tasks, Right to Rent checks, provider onboarding, and best practices. Refer back to these videos whenever you need a refresher, and remember — consistency, documentation, and clear communication are the foundations of great property management.

Thank you for watching.
